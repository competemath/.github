# Code of Conduct

We aim to foster compassion for one another, by helping and teaching each other useful knowledge. Our [purpose](PURPOSE.md) and the values we try to live by are set out separately; this code says how conduct is judged and what happens when it falls short.

## Our pledge

We pledge a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation. We judge conduct, and the intent it plainly shows, in our spaces, not people's identity, beliefs or way of life.

## What we expect

Clear expectations are what make a welcoming space possible. Every interaction in this organisation and its projects is judged only by its content, and by the intent it plainly shows or that convincing, reviewed evidence supports, and never because of a person's identity, beliefs or way of life. Interactions with or within the organisation that are not relevant to its work are out of scope here and may be moved or removed, for the sake of focus.

Intent is read in good faith first. A mistake, a clumsy joke or a first off-topic post gets a private correction and a chance to reply. Conduct that plainly shows an intent to harm, harass, disrupt or campaign, such as repeating it after being asked to stop, or code that looks built to weaken the project, is treated as that intent. The same standard applies to everyone.

## What this looks like

- We keep our spaces about the work. Advocating or campaigning for causes unrelated to the project, whatever the cause and whichever side, does not belong here, and neither does debating them.
- We do not ask people about themselves, and we do not press anyone to share personal information.
- We do not publish other people's private information.
- We treat each other with respect: no insults, trolling, personal attacks, harassment, or sexualised language and unwelcome attention.
- We do not harm the project or the people in it, or set out to: no threats, no malicious code, no sabotage, no contributions meant to weaken it.
- We credit other people's work.

## Maintainers' responsibilities

Maintainers are bound by this code first. They explain what is expected and act when it is not met. They may edit, move, lock or remove comments, issues, discussions and contributions that do not follow this code, and block accounts, and they say why when they do; you may reply. If you need something in order to take part, such as an accessibility adjustment, tell a maintainer; it will be handled privately.

## Scope

This code applies to conduct in every CompeteMath repository and discussion space, and to anyone representing the project in public. What people do or believe elsewhere is not our concern unless it is aimed at the project's spaces or the people in them.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour privately on GitHub: open the Security tab of competemath/tengoku and choose "Report a vulnerability". The report stays between you and the maintainers.

## Enforcement

Maintainers respond in proportion, in this order where it fits: a private correction, a warning, a temporary ban from the project's spaces, a permanent ban. A serious breach can skip steps.
