# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

CompeteMath's high-level purpose is to be the knowledge, security, and reliability layer for agentic formal theorem provers. The lower-level purpose that this stands on is the pursuit of knowledge for the sake of God (في سبيل الله):

> "Whoever takes a path in which he seeks knowledge, Allah will make easy for him, by it, a path to Paradise."
>
> «ومن سلك طريقا يلتمس فيه علما سهل الله له به طريقا إلى الجنة»
>
> — Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, no. 2699 (narrated by Abū Hurayrah), Kitāb al-Dhikr wa-l-Duʿāʾ. Manuscript copy of 1164 CE: [Princeton University Library, Garrett MS 104Y, fol. 167a](https://dpul.princeton.edu/islamicmss/catalog/cr56n359w), [exact page](https://iiif-cloud.princeton.edu/iiif/2/4b%2F29%2F26%2F4b2926c452bf475ba690d038a6577e8b%2Fintermediate_file/full/full/0/default.jpg).

The hadith speaks of seeking ʿilm, which in its classical setting is most often understood as religious knowledge. Scholars have also discussed whether beneficial worldly knowledge counts, and treated mastery of such fields by some members of the community as a communal obligation (fard kifāya) that becomes worship when pursued sincerely for God. We hold that reading: our work is beneficial knowledge pursued with that intention. This is our reading and not a ruling, and it is open to correction (see "Raising concerns about this Code").

Our purpose is rooted in Islam. We do not ask contributors to share that faith or any other belief, and we do not use the organisation to argue for beliefs, lifestyles or causes, ours or anyone else's, that are unrelated to the work. This statement of purpose is the one exception: it is the organisation saying what it is, and is not advocacy within the work. Contributors may identify, believe and live however they want. What we require is that interactions and contributions in the organisation, including any of its projects, now or later, are in line with the purpose above, as non-exhaustively elaborated below. This applies to people and to automated systems alike; whoever causes even a single line of code to run is held responsible for what it does.

Hosting someone's contribution or account is not an endorsement of the person's views or way of life, and nothing here claims to settle any question of Islamic law or speak for Muslims in general. The standard we hold ourselves to is the purpose above.

Only what happens in the organisation's own spaces is judged. What a contributor says, believes or does elsewhere, including in a profile or a campaign outside the organisation, is not a reason to act against them, and neither is a name, a pronoun or the people named in an example. If their conduct here is in line with the purpose, they are welcome. An example in documentation or a docstring is judged on whether it is clear and correct.

## Being in line with our purpose

This looks like:

- Claiming only what you have checked, understood well enough to defend, and have had reviewed.
- Not deceiving fellow contributors and members of the organisation.
- Doing any work you take part in to the best of your ability.
- Helping and teaching, crediting others, interacting well-mannered, and leaving things better than you found them.
- Do not intentionally harm or attack any project, the people who use it, or the people working on it. Security research done for beneficial knowledge in a non disruptive manner is not considered an attack.
- Keep self-promotion, quarrels, advocacy, and sharing of anything unrelated out of work. This applies equally to every topic and every viewpoint.

Contributions and interactions are judged against this by their content and the intent supported by materially convincing and reviewed evidence, but never by anyone's identity, beliefs or way of life.

## Scope

This applies in every CompeteMath repository, discussion, issue, pull request, and any other space where someone acts for or in the name of the organisation.

## Reporting and enforcement

- Report a concern privately to a maintainer or an organisation owner (GitHub's "Report content", or a private message). Do not start a public thread about it.
- A first slip is met with good faith: assume it was not meant to cause trouble, point to the rule, and ask for the post to be edited. For example, someone who shares a personal detail that has nothing to do with the work is asked to remove it. A repeated pattern of unrelated personal detail, or unrelated questions about other people's private lives, is enforced.
- Maintainers respond in proportion: a reminder or a request to edit first, then removal of content, then a temporary or permanent block. A serious deliberate harm may skip steps.
- The person concerned is told what rule was involved and why, and may reply once and ask for a second maintainer or owner to review the decision. Reports are kept confidential.
- Maintainers who are involved in a matter do not decide it.

## Raising concerns about this Code

Anyone may ask, politely and in the open or privately, whether this Code is right or whether a particular reading of Islam in it is wrong. Such a question is on topic. Maintainers look into it against the sources cited and amend the Code if it turns out to be mistaken.
